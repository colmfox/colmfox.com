For news publishers

If you have arrived here from your server logs, thank you for checking. This page explains who is visiting your site and why, how the data is used, what the visitor does and has collected, and how to stop it.

Academic, non-commercial research. Articles are never republished, and the data is never sold or used for any commercial purpose.

Who this is

The visitor is the collector for SEAPED, the Southeast Asia Protest Event Database. SEAPED is a non-commercial academic research project led by Colm A. Fox, Associate Professor of Political Science at Singapore Management University (faculty profile on the SMU website · personal website). It looks for news reports of protests in order to build a research record of protest in Southeast Asia.

The collector identifies itself in every request with this user-agent string:

SEAPEDbot/0.1 (+https://colmfox.com/collector/seapedbot/; colmfox@smu.edu.sg)

Other software can copy this name. If SEAPEDbot behaves differently from what this page describes, it may not be us: email colmfox@smu.edu.sg and we will check our records.

Why it matters

Much of the protest in Southeast Asia is reported only in local and national news, and your reporting is often the only record that a protest took place. SEAPED turns that reporting into a research record, so that these protests are counted when scholars study protest across the region, not only the ones that reach the international press.

Each event names the outlet that reported it and links to the original article, so readers who want the full story are sent to your reporting.

How the data is used

SEAPED is academic, non-commercial research. The coded data is used in research publications. It is never sold or used for any commercial purpose.

SEAPED is independent. It is not commissioned by, and does not work for, any government, police or security agency. It is not a monitoring tool: it records protests only from news that has already been published, does not follow social media, and does not track individuals. Private individuals are recorded only as counts and roles, never by name, and minors are never named. The released data comes with a condition that it may not be used to harm, target or oppress the people or groups it records.

Funding: SEAPED currently has no external grant funding. Any funder will be named here.

What it does on your site

  • It reads robots.txt on every visit and follows it.
  • It visits once a day, makes one request at a time, pauses between requests, and stops if your site signals that it is busy.
  • It reads lists of new articles from your own feeds and sitemaps, and downloads only articles that appear to report a protest.
  • It never uses proxies, never disguises itself, and never gets around a paywall or login. It logs in only where a publisher has agreed to this.

What it has collected

SEAPED began a test run in November 2026, reading headlines only. Counts for each outlet will be published here once collection is under way.

What happens to your articles

  • Articles are stored on the researcher’s own computer, with encrypted backups.
  • They are read by an AI model that runs on that computer. Article text is never sent to cloud AI services.
  • Their text, headlines and summaries of them are never republished or sold, and are never shared outside the research team.
  • Only coded information about each protest, such as its date, place and form, is ever made public, together with a link to the original article.

How to stop it

Email colmfox@smu.edu.sg and the collector will stop visiting your site within 24 hours. You can also ask for it to collect less.

Or block it in your robots.txt. It will stop on its next visit:

User-agent: SEAPEDbot
Disallow: /
