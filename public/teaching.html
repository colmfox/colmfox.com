Teaching

I teach courses on politics, the media, elections, and social movements at Singapore Management University, with an emphasis on content analysis and survey research.

Current courses

POSC108

AI-Driven Media Research

Artificial intelligence is transforming how researchers analyse the media. In this project-based course we ask why the media focus on certain issues, amplify particular voices, and frame stories as they do, across elections, protests, conflicts, and issues such as climate change and human rights. Students learn traditional content analysis (sampling, variable definition, codebook construction, and manual coding for validation), then use Python and free AI models to analyse thousands of articles. Working in groups on topics of their choosing, they produce a poster presentation and an original research paper.

POSC304

Social Movements in Asia

Syllabus

Following the recent surge in activism across Asia, particularly among young people, in this course we examine the origins, dynamics, and impacts of contemporary social movements. Through social movement theory, case studies, and documentary screenings, we ask what drives mobilization, how political, social, and cultural contexts shape movements, and what challenges activists face. Cases include pro-democracy movements in Hong Kong, Thailand, and Myanmar; civil rights in the Philippines; labour in Thailand; environmental movements in China and Singapore; and the repression and surveillance of movements in China.

Past courses

POSC322

Mass Media and Public Opinion Research

Why does the media focus on certain issues, give voice to particular actors, and frame stories in particular ways, and how does this shape public opinion and behaviour? In this project-based research design course, I teach students to write a rigorous empirical paper, to survey public opinion, and to code media content systematically, from sampling and codebook construction to designing and assessing questionnaires. Working in groups on topics of their choosing, students design either a content analysis or a survey, and present an original research paper of about 9,000 words.

POSC320

Elections and the Media

Elections are arguably the most fundamental aspect of democracy, and around the world more people are voting and engaging with campaigns through the media. In this course we explore how campaigns unfold through the media, why people vote the way they do, and how news and campaign advertising affect voting behaviour, with examples from the US, Latin America, Europe, Africa, and Asia, and from traditional media as well as websites and social networks.

POSC003

Introduction to Politics and Policy Studies

In this introduction to political science we cover the formation of the nation-state, authoritarianism, democratization, ideology, political culture, institutions, conflict, development, and globalization. Why are some countries more democratic than others? Can civil society affect political change? What causes war? Students learn the main concepts and approaches in the field and apply them to recent events, with a particular focus on Asia.
